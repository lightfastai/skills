# Developing an article

Use for research, engineering, method, company approach and product articles. These are flexible editing questions, not mandatory headings or minimum length requirements.

Start with one sentence for the reader's question and one for the claim. Identify available records, an example linking evidence to a decision, and the important unresolved limitation. Choose a golden reference whose explanatory job matches the piece.

## Build depth around a decision

- **Question and claim:** who encounters the problem, and what narrow conclusion can this piece support?
- **Mechanism:** what happens causally, what persists, who/what chooses the next step, and where does human judgment enter?
- **Example:** follow an episode through question, intervention, output, evaluation and next decision. Put the artifact where the reader would otherwise have to trust the prose. Label proposed examples in place.
- **Evidence:** include baseline, versions, setup, selection, budget and measurement conditions that affect interpretation. Separate exploration from confirmation. Explain invalid runs, failures, exclusions, variation and denominators when relevant. Multiple simultaneous changes limit causal attribution.
- **Limits and consequence:** state untested conditions, error sources and alternative explanations beside the affected claim. End with the practical implication or next observation that would change the conclusion.

An intended process establishes aspiration; an inspectable component establishes implementation; a test establishes an observation under its conditions; broader conclusions need repeated, relevant evidence. None automatically entails the next.

For continual research, show what was learned between experiments and why the next hypothesis followed. Retain failed alternatives and stopping reasons where they explain the search. A static architecture picture is insufficient evidence of behavior over time.

## Select a useful visual

| Job | Useful form | Inspectable content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mechanism | Process/state diagram | Inputs, retained state, decisions and owners |
| Decision | Annotated experiment trace | Evidence, interpretation and next action |
| Comparison | Plot or compact table | Baseline, units, conditions, variation and relevant cost |
| Provenance | Evidence lineage | Claim, artifact and how it was produced |
| Behavior over time | Sequence/demo | Meaningful transitions and failures |

## Optional review rubric

Score each dimension 0 (missing), 1 (unclear), or 2 (explicit and inspectable) when comparing substantive revisions: claim, mechanism, example, evidence, limits, consequence. Do not impose a scorecard on every draft. An unsupported central claim requires revision regardless of total.

To expand a short draft, identify what each paragraph does: claim, mechanism, example, evidence, limitation or implication. Missing explanation suggests useful expansion; repeated promises suggest padding. Perspective essays may develop an argument and objection instead of experimental evidence. Product pieces need a real usage path and availability; they do not need fabricated benchmarks.
